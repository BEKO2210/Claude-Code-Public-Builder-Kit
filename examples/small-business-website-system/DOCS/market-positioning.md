# Market positioning — Website System for Small Local Businesses

How we describe this product to the world, and how we tell it apart from alternatives in **small business**.

## 1. Positioning statement

> For **small local businesses** who need to perform a daily core action without ceremony, **Website System for Small Local Businesses** is a focused website that gets out of your way. Unlike generic tools and bloated incumbents, it does one thing well, exports your data on demand, and is small enough to learn in an afternoon.

## 2. Target segment (first 100 users)

- **Who:** small local businesses, owner-operators or first hires, working in **small business**.
- **Where:** wherever they already gather online (forum, subreddit, Discord, Slack community, in-person meetups).
- **Pain trigger:** spending more time wrangling tools than doing the actual work.
- **Buying authority:** themselves. No procurement.

## 3. Competitive frame

Competitors tend to fall into three archetypes. Be explicit about which one you are *not*:

| Archetype | Examples | Why users dislike it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The bloated incumbent** | Enterprise suites that have absorbed every adjacent feature. | Slow, expensive, hard to learn, full of things you don't need. |
| **The generic horizontal tool** | Spreadsheets, generic project management apps. | Powerful but agnostic — every team rebuilds the same workflow. |
| **The half-finished startup** | Last year's "we're disrupting X" launch. | Pretty landing page, broken product, no follow-through. |

Our differentiation:

- **Domain-specific.** Built for the exact daily core action, not for everything in small business.
- **Boring tech.** Reliability over novelty.
- **Portable by default.** CSV export from day one. No data hostages.
- **Small surface area.** A new user can learn it in under an hour.

## 4. Anti-positioning (what we will not be)

- We will not be the cheapest. Cheapness implies cutting corners we will not cut.
- We will not be the most configurable. Configuration is a confession that we don't know our user.
- We will not be the AI tool. We use AI when it makes the daily core action faster — not as a brand.

## 5. Messaging hierarchy

| Level | Audience | Message |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Headline** | Stranger landing on the homepage | "The fastest way for small local businesses to <do the core action>." |
| **Sub-headline** | Same | "Built specifically for small business. No setup. No lock-in." |
| **Hero proof** | Same | One short demo or screenshot of the daily core action. |
| **Pricing line** | Considering signup | A single, legible price (or "free while in beta"). |
| **Onboarding line** | First minute in-app | "You can do <core action> right now. We'll get out of your way." |

Replace the bracketed phrasing once `MASTERPLAN.md` names the daily core action precisely.

## 6. Distribution hypotheses (test in order)

1. **Direct outreach** to 20 candidates from the target segment. Are they willing to try a working version?
2. **Community presence** in the two highest-density places small local businesses already gather. Are they engaging?
3. **Content** — one piece per month answering a real question they search for. Does it bring qualified signups?
4. **Paid acquisition** — only after 1–3 demonstrably work. Otherwise we are paying to fill a leaky bucket.

## 7. Risks to positioning

- **Scope creep:** we drift toward "platform" and lose the focused promise.
- **Vague language:** marketing copy that sounds clever but doesn't describe what the product does.
- **Imitation:** an incumbent ships a similar feature; we must lean harder on opinionation, not match feature counts.
